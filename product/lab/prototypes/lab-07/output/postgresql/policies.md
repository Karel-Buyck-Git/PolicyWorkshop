# PostgreSQL Policies

## Tier Rationale

**Essential — TLS in transit, Entra ID admin, geo-redundant backup.**
PostgreSQL Flexible Server typically holds relational data of record — customer accounts, transactions, application state — so the baseline removes the most common compromise paths: SSL enforced on every connection, TLS 1.2+ minimum, a Microsoft Entra administrator provisioned so AD authentication is available rather than only local accounts, and geo-redundant backup enabled at server creation (it cannot be added later). These map to ISO 27001 A.8.24 (cryptography in transit), A.5.15 (access control), and A.5.30/A.8.13 (backup and recovery), with NIS2 Article 21(2)(c) business continuity expectations.

**Professional — pgAudit and structured server-level logging.**
At this tier the pgAudit extension is preloaded and configured (role, DDL, misc events), `log_connections`/`log_disconnections`/`log_checkpoints` are turned on, `log_line_prefix` is set with the user/database/session identifiers needed for correlation, and log retention is set to at least 7 days. These are observability controls — someone needs to review or alert on the resulting logs — which is what places them at Professional. They support ISO 27001 A.8.15 (logging) and CIS PostgreSQL Benchmark logging recommendations.

**Enterprise — Diagnostic-settings pipeline, private endpoints, customer-managed keys.**
Enterprise customers stream the diagnostic settings into a Log Analytics workspace (so logs feed a SIEM rather than just sit on the server), require private endpoints for all connectivity (no public route), and encrypt at rest with customer-managed keys held in Key Vault with controlled rotation. This combination meets ISO 27001 A.10/A.8.24 (cryptography) and A.12.4 (logging and monitoring), and the data-sovereignty and incident-reporting requirements of NIS2 Articles 21(2)(h) and 23 — typical for regulated relational workloads in finance, healthcare, and EU public sector.
